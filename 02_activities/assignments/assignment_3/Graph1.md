- Graph 1:
    > Python was used to creat this visualization since I feel that it is the most powerful and flexible at generating visualizations if/when I need to process data (i.e., add errorbars, give means or medians, etc.)

    > Intended audience are pig farmers/ people who want data on when to buy/sell pigs
    
    > For this visualization, I aim for a more rational approach, focusing more on accurately presenting data to determine when it would be best to sell a pig rather than emotional or moral appeals/focusing on representation.
    
    > I mainly focused on presenting Return on investment values as they relate to the breakeven point of a $0 return. This way, a farmer can, at a glance, determine which months would yeild a net profit and which would leave them at a loss. This is important for my target audience as they would value maximizing profit. To do this, I added clear indicators of this breakeven point in both subplots, either as a divergent colourbar with white/zero in the middle, or a line at 0 for a line plot, so readers can easiely see which values are above/below it.
    
    > I used python and included all commented code and data used to generate my visualization in this repository.
    
    > I ensured that all title text is >12ppt, and axis text is >10ppt. Unfortunately, divergent colourmaps cannot be easiely used for people with achromatopsia, but I ensured that it was accessible for other types of colourblindness using the Rd_Bu colourmap in python.
    
    > Other than the target audience of people in the pork industry, this visualization can also impact animal rights and climate groups, since it encourages a view on pig farming to optimize efficeincy and profit rather than sustainability and wellbeing of the pigs.

    > Since I wanted to focus susinctly on which months would give the most profit to inform farmers when to sell their pigs, I ignored most data relating to other factors that influence return on investment. This allows for a clearer message about optimal selling time, which is easiely controlable, over factors outside of a farmer's control such as feed price, and other pig maintainance costs.
    
    > Underwater labour includes the OMAFA and ONswine, which keeps track of databases on monthly costs of swine, and individual farmers, butchers, and other pig industry employees who are responsible for collecting data.
