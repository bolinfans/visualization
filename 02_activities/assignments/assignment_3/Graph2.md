- Graph 2:
    > Libre office and Google docs was used to creat this visualization. I did not like using it as I prefer the flexibility of python, but both are very accessible.

    > Intended audience are people concerned about trends in farming in modern years.
    
    > For this visualization, I aim for a moral/emotional approach, focusing more on representing a narritive that pork farming is encouraging increases in pig size. This comes at the cost that it is harder to read exact values in the visualization than with Graph 1, and also introducing some redundance with marker size and y axis representing the same thing.
    
    > For this visualization, I focus on telling a narritive; that over the years, we have been making pigs progressively bigger, to the detriment of the pigs' health. This is represented by my choice of graph stype; a scatter plot of pig mass to year with the marker size corresponding to the size of pig, with a size of 1 representing the smallest mean pig size year in the sample. This makes the graph a bit more redundent. However, this lets the points in the scatter plot 'balloon' as pig mass increases, showing that this trend of raising larger pigs may have a negative effect on pig health.
    
    > I include in a spreadsheet values for the marker size and marker value for each marker plotted in my scatter plot.
    
    > I ensured that all title text is >12ppt, and axis text is >10ppt. Since this visualization focuses more on telling a story than presenting data efficiently, it is easier to read its message at a glance, and I did not need to make too many tradeoffs in accessibility by making the plot more complicated.
    
    > People this visualization may affect include members of the general public who are concerned about modern agricultural practices and animal welfare advocates, who may find it useful as a communication tool to highlight how modern farming has led to progressively larger pigs, potentially at the expense of animal health and well-being

    > I chose to focus on year and pig mass (dressed weight) because these variables most directly support the narrative I wanted to communicate: that pigs have been getting progressively larger over time. Other available variables, such as feed costs, market value, or net return, were excluded because they would shift the focus toward economic efficiency rather than animal outcomes
